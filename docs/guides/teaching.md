# Make the mechanism visible

Start from the question the viewer should be able to answer. Adapt the explanation to their existing knowledge. A business audience is not a reason to avoid technical ideas; explain the technical step with a small concrete case.

A useful arc is question → worked example → mechanism → changed case → limit. It is a pattern, not a fixed scene count. Do not force every subject into a dramatic cold open or a marketing pitch.

For each scene, ask what the picture explains that the narration alone does not. Use a comparison, relationship, transformation, flow or concrete object. Large animated headings by themselves rarely explain a mechanism.

Keep claims, examples, metaphors and assumptions distinct. Label synthetic numbers. State where an analogy stops working. Cite the actual source of a number or result, and keep unresolved claims visible in source notes rather than polishing away uncertainty.

Narration should sound natural at its normal speed. Use short spoken sentences and room to think. Add unfamiliar names to the pronunciation lexicon. Listen to numbers, acronyms and proper nouns. Do not remove all pauses to meet a duration target.

Bottom captions use short phrases and a highlighted active word. Caption text is not another slide title. Use plain or clean output when motion distracts or the context calls for it. Show long formulas and precise numeric values in the diagram while speaking them naturally.

Before calling it done, ask whether a viewer could explain the mechanism back and predict a changed example. These are review questions; do not claim a learning benefit you have not observed.

Use [scene direction](scene-direction.md) when writing or revising scenes: plan the visible operation and its invariant before choosing animation.

## A practical scene review

Before production, name the recurring objects, units and the visual change that explains each step. Use the same identity across scenes. Tie reveals to speech cues and reserve bottom space for captions. Ask the learner to predict one changed case before showing its answer. Describe the meaningful visual relationship in narration or an authored visual description. See [the engine decision](../design/engine-choice.md) before proposing a new renderer.
